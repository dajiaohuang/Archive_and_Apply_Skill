# Role-Specific Resume/CV Audit

Use this reference to select and order evidence for a target role. Do not maintain a universal catalog of job titles; derive criteria from the actual JD or stated direction.

## Inputs

- target JD or role definition
- locale and document type: industry resume, academic CV, federal/portal form, or other
- page/file constraints from the employer
- current source entries, entry bank, and target document
- likely review path: parser/ATS, recruiter, hiring manager/team leader, technical reviewers, peers, cross-functional partners, or committee

If no JD exists, state the role assumptions used for the audit.

## Reader-path hypothesis

Do not assume every organization uses the same sequence. Record which readers are likely, which are confirmed, and what decision each one must make. Prioritize the earliest gate without starving later reviewers of credible depth.

- **parser/ATS:** supported terminology and recoverable structure
- **recruiter/HR:** recognizable fit, level, scope, and constraints
- **hiring manager/team leader:** predicted performance, judgment, ownership, and impact
- **technical interviewer:** depth, tradeoffs, constraints, evaluation, and probe-worthy specifics
- **potential peer/cross-functional interviewer:** collaboration interfaces, quality practices, communication, and team leverage
- **committee/senior leader when relevant:** trajectory, organizational scope, multiplier effect, and risk judgment

Treat these as reader needs, not assumptions about an individual's technical ability.

## Requirement extraction

Separate target signals into:

- **must-have:** explicit minimum qualifications or central responsibilities
- **preferred:** differentiators, not eligibility gates
- **domain:** product, users, data, regulations, or research context
- **execution:** shipping, experimentation, collaboration, operations, or communication
- **constraints:** location, authorization, degree, graduation date, language, portfolio, file format

Do not treat repeated marketing language as a hard requirement without evidence from the posting.

## Entry scoring

Score each candidate entry from 0 to 3 on:

- **relevance:** direct match to central role work
- **evidence:** strength and verifiability of outcomes
- **ownership:** clarity of the candidate's contribution
- **recency:** current enough to represent capability
- **differentiation:** adds a signal not already covered better elsewhere
- **reader coverage:** gives the likely review path useful, non-duplicative evidence from accessible orientation through defensible depth

Use the scores to support judgment, not as an automatic ranking formula. A mandatory credential or uniquely relevant project can override totals.

## Decision labels

- **must keep:** strongest evidence for a central target signal
- **keep if space:** useful but redundant or secondary
- **rewrite:** relevant evidence exists but wording hides it
- **omit for this target:** weak relevance or displaced by stronger evidence
- **evidence gap:** the JD asks for a signal the archive does not substantiate

Never convert an evidence gap into a claim.

## Skills section

Keep a skill when it is important to the target and the resume contains defensible evidence for it. A one-off library may belong only inside a project bullet. Group skills by useful categories, not by perceived prestige.

## Ordering and length

- Follow explicit employer requirements first.
- Put the highest-value evidence where it is most likely to be read.
- Use reverse chronology within a section unless a different structure better serves the requested format.
- Do not stretch a sparse document or shrink typography to hit an arbitrary page count.
- Distinguish an academic CV, which may be comprehensive, from an industry resume, which is selective.
- Put an accessible overview before niche implementation detail, then use later bullets for decisions, validation, and team leverage.
- Ensure each major claim can seed a truthful interview discussion about ownership, alternatives, measurement, failure, and collaborators.

## Output contract

Produce:

1. target and assumptions
2. must-have / preferred / constraint summary
3. reader-path hypothesis and decision needs
4. requirement-to-evidence matrix with source paths
5. keep / rewrite / omit and reader-coverage decisions
6. Skills-section decisions
7. evidence gaps and likely interview probes
8. page/file-format recommendation
9. exact downstream files to change
